# Audit of the stream maps, 2026-11-22

**What was audited.** The live site at commit `d16a885` (published 2026-11-21 23:47, all 12 areas), the code that
built it (`8bdf945` on main), the docs, and the site as it stood before that publish (`6875118`) for comparison.
Numbers are for the live site unless they say otherwise; its 12 `map3d_data.js` files are byte-for-byte the build I
measured.

**How.** I measured what the maps state against data in the project that the value was not derived from: NHD's
stream lines, USGS gauge records and their site drainage areas, NHDPlus, the trout survey records, measured water
temperatures, the known-falls lists, NHD's lakes. Five read-only helpers read all the code and docs; I spot-checked
what they found before using it. Nothing in `scripts/`, `data/`, `output/` or `publish/` was changed; nothing was
built, committed or pushed. Scripts, tables and the helpers' notes are in `audit/` (section 7).

---

## 1. Summary

**What is right.** The streams are where they should be. Every named creek NHD maps that drains 0.39 sq mi or more is
drawn, a median 23-36 ft from NHD's line. Names agree with NHD on 99.6-99.9 % of miles. Drainage areas match USGS
gauges within 10 % at 84 % of 499 gauges. Elevation, canopy and sun hours in the popups reproduce their sources, the
fish score reproduces from its own table, and the "± °F" on water temperature is an honest error figure. No script
calls USGS WaterServices any more.

**What is not**, most important first: can it be reached, does it hold water, is the creek there; then the score,
the code, the site and the paperwork. The table is the one-page version; the same fifteen follow in a few lines each.

**1. Approach times and scores carry the build day's road closures, winter gates included.** Jackson, built
21 November: 666 of 10,578 reaches (557 at fish 60+) start at a winter gate; Cascade Creek (43.76524, -110.74842)
reads "park at Teton Park Road → bike 57 min on closed roads → hike 48 min". The Smokies have five park roads shut for
the winter, Vermont and Lexington one each. Remoteness is a quarter of the score, so those reaches also score high.
*Where:* `08_hike.py:64-68`, `09_drive.py:58-63`; the `long_term` flag `07_road_closures.py` makes is read by nothing.
*Fix:* time the approach on the open-season network and show today's closures as a dated note (decision 1).
*Rebuild from* `08_hike`.

**2. Outside LA the summer-flow class cannot say anything but "year-round".** It is NHDPlus's mean flow times a
yearly index, which has no drying in it: in effect a drainage cut at 0.12-0.27 sq mi, under the map's own floor.
94-99.6 % of drawn miles read year-round on ten maps; NHD calls 46 % of Jackson's year-round miles intermittent or
unmapped, 39 % of Lexington's, 32 % of the High Peaks'. In NE Connecticut the cut sits at 0.93 sq mi and works the
other way: 669 NHD-perennial reaches (439 mi), 398 with trout on record, read below year-round and lose score. The cfs
figure runs 3x high on small Vermont creeks and 7x in Tallulah Gorge. *Where:* `02b_download_flow.py:201-232`,
`05b_flow.py:470-477`, `flowlib.py:369-384`. *Fix:* keep the class (it errs the way you asked) but show NHD's doubt
beside it; lift NHD-perennial creeks in Connecticut and Vermont (decision 2). *Rebuild from* `05b_flow`.

**3. In LA, where the class does carry information, it contradicts trout records and flips along one river.** Trout
were recorded since 2016 on 81 reaches (53 mi) shown mostly dry or seasonal (Arroyo Trabuco, 33.6005, -117.6313:
"mostly dry, 0.0 cfs"). 145 mi NHD maps perennial read mostly dry. The bare-channel rule is decided reach by reach:
the Santa Clara River flips about twenty times (205 sudden changes, 90 mi). The cfs figure is within 2x of the gauge
at 46 % of 28 gauges. *Where:* `scoring.py:224-237`. *Fix:* a trout record lifts a reach to "usually flows"; decide
the bare-channel rule per creek between confluences (decision 3). *Rebuild* LA *from* `05b_flow`.

**4. Rivers whose headwaters lie outside the map are drawn too small.** Against USGS gauges the popup's drainage is
37 % short on the Chattooga at Burrells Ford (29 against 47 sq mi), 59 % on the French River (CT), 35 % on Vermont's
Green River, 25 % on the Independence. At 169 of 4,806 NHDPlus points of 39 sq mi or more no drawn line has half the
drainage (LA 81, Jackson 19, Adirondacks south-east 19, NE Connecticut 12). *Where:* `crossbox.py:30`, `:451`, `:586`
(an outside river must bring 3.9 sq mi and a quarter of the total). *Fix:* carry NHDPlus's total at every stream
crossing the outer edge. *Rebuild from* `04_knickpoints` (`SKIP_03B=1`).

**5. The 0.39 sq mi floor leaves out named brook-trout headwaters.** 2,894 of 23,242 named NHD stream miles are not
drawn: 26 % in the Smokies (about 300 named branches missing whole), 12-13 % in North Georgia and NE Connecticut.
98 of 2,880 trout-found records sit on undrawn water. *Where:* `05_score.py:12`. *Fix:* your call (decision 4).
*Rebuild from* `05_score`.

**6. The drawing step still rewrites the streams, so map and tables disagree.** `06_map.py` clips 15,160 reaches at
lake shores, hides 8,614, adds 1,138 lines (137 mi), re-values 1,123 pieces, raises the order of 9,122 and bridges
949 dams. Tables: 56,033 mi, 26,697 at fish 60+; map: 50,526 and 25,112. None of this went away with the root fixes
of 21 November. *Where:* `06_map.py:84-283`, `:687-945`; behind it a one-cell gap at every lake outlet
(`03b_stitch.py:428`, `:578`) and two different sets of lakes (`flowlib.py:187-207` against `05d_lakes.py:42`).
*Fix:* section 3.1. *Rebuild from* `03b_stitch` (`SKIP_03=1`).

**7. The fish score marks water down on evidence that is not near-certain.** One empty sample within 0.9 mi halves a
reach even inside designated trout water: 267 reaches, 117 of them designated (`scoring.py:199`). A failed flow
look-up scores "unmapped" 0.15: 364 reaches outside LA at 8-20 beside neighbours at 60+ (`scoring.py:224-226`). An
unmapped burn severity counts as the worst: 3,462 of 4,061 burned reaches (`scoring.py:124`). The new lake term takes
10+ points from 133 mi of drawn Adirondack creek, 125 mi of it with a trout record; measured sites support it within
650 ft of the pond (29 mi) and about half of it further down (section 2.6). Outside LA the score is mostly stream size, remoteness and whether
a record exists. *Fix:* decision 5. *Rebuild from* `06_map`.

**8. Two lake-routing faults are live** (found by the other sessions, still open). 31 lakes put their water back in
650 ft or more from the outlet (Seventh Lake's outlet creek is not drawn); 77 lakes drain to a cell 1,000 ft or more
from NHD's outlet (Raquette Lake 5.7 mi). *Where:* `crossbox.py:262`, `:335`; `03b_stitch.py:552-556`. *Fix:* take
the outlet from NHD's line leaving the lake; same batch as 6. *Rebuild from* `03b_stitch`.

**9. A new area does not "just work".** It needs hand-laid boxes whose edges dodge lakes, four tables keyed by area
(`02f_known_falls.py:45`, `05d_lakes.py:42`, `lake_bathymetry.py:424`, `scoring.py:153`), per-state adapters, 12 name
overrides (`05c_drainages.py:46-76`), and caches cleared by hand after an edge moves (15 families keyed by box name).
17 rules were made for one named place and never measured on all areas. LA wording ships elsewhere: 178 New York
trail pieces read "CA State Parks". *Fix:* section 4. *No rebuild* until the pieces land.

**10. Nothing ties a published map to the code that built it, and failures pass quietly.** Tonight two scripts were
edited while areas were building, every map was drawn again by hand outside the logs, and the code was committed
after the build; it came out consistent, but only file times show it. Two drivers carry on after a failed hydrology step
(`run_base.sh:19-20`, `run_rebuild_a.sh:22-23`); an Overpass time-out is cached as a full answer (`geomatch.py:18`).
*Fix:* a build stamp in every output; publish refuses mixed or uncommitted builds. *No rebuild.*

**11. Licences.** New York DEC's ArcGIS layers are on the four Adirondack pages (unlinked, public), against your
data.ny.gov rule (`troutdata.py:490-527`, `geomatch.py:76`). 222 falls from two sites that forbid redistribution
await consent; 695 from a site with no licence went out without asking its author; about 85 more have no terms on
record. Sentinel-2 is uncredited. *Fix:* decision 6. *Rebuild from* `02f_known_falls` or `11_context` if pulled.

**12. The website can lose about a third of its size with nothing removed.** 636 MiB of 1 GB. 62 MB is three
sentences on every reach that hold 9 MB of numbers; the 3D page (114 MB) is nearly all a second copy of layers the
2D page carries; 13 MB is reaches written twice. *Where:* `06_map.py:953-992`, `:1221-1223`, `:1531-1538`.
*Fix:* section 3.7. *Rebuild from* `06_map`.

**13. The checks disagree with each other and read the tables, not the map; the README's build recipe is broken.**
"0 flow drops in all 12" and seven flow drops were logged the same day by different scripts; I count 269 sudden
changes on the drawn maps. `README.md:651-659` fails on a clean checkout; 14 statements are wrong today; the log is
314 KB with 39 open items scattered through it. *Fix:* a standing set of checks on the drawn data (3.6); a short
state file (3.8). *No rebuild.*

**14. A batch of small wrong labels.** In the Adirondacks south-east 520 of the 564 lines draining 100 sq mi or
more are "order 7" and order 6 is 11 small creeks (`06_map.py:98-101`); "hike -0 min" on 142 reaches; "Outlet creek profile" opens nothing for 3,601 of 7,450 lakes
(`maplakes.py:225`); the dry-lake edge never draws (`maplakes.py:253`); five Wyoming streams the state lists as
troutless are labelled native cutthroat water (`troutdata.py:262-264`); gauge times are Pacific on every map.
*Fix:* one commit. *Rebuild from* `11_context`.

**15. Three things could not be checked at all.** Drive and hike times (nothing independent in the project); drop
heights and whether an "unmapped" drop is real (the terrain alone finds 44-76 % of known falls); whether a small
creek outside LA holds water in August. Section 2 says what data would settle each.

## 2. Accuracy by topic

Each topic says what was verified right and how, what is wrong, and what could not be checked and what data would
settle it. Tables are per area; `adk_*` are the four hidden Adirondack maps.

### 2.1 Is the creek there, and in the right place

**Right.**
- Named creeks. I walked all 23,242 mi of NHD's named stream lines inside the maps in 80 ft steps and looked for a
  drawn line within 500 ft. The 2,894 mi with nothing drawn all lie where the terrain routing has under 0.39 sq mi,
  the map's size floor. No named creek above the floor is missing, apart from three kinds left out on purpose: New
  York's shore inside the Vermont boxes (cut at the state line), the centrelines of lakes cut by a map edge (Tugaloo
  11 mi, Tellico 9.8 mi) and tidal water (Darby Creek 5 mi, the Delaware, the Christina, the lower Schuylkill).
- Course. Where NHD has a line within 500 ft, the drawn line sits a median 23-36 ft from it, and 90 % of it within
  69-138 ft.

**Wrong, or worth knowing.**
- The floor hides named headwaters (finding 5; the 98 trout records on undrawn water are in
  `trout_records_off_map.csv`).
- 3,472 of 50,526 drawn miles (6.9 %) have no NHD line of any kind within 500 ft, 741 mi of them rated fish 60+
  (High Peaks 199, Jackson 135, Adirondacks north-west 133, south-west 127, south-east 77). Nearly all are order 1-2.
  Their popups give a modelled year-round flow; only 5-20 mi per map carry the "NHD unmapped" note. They may be real
  brooks NHD never drew or lines across wet flats. The 796 longest are in `streams_vs_nhd_extra.csv`; three to look
  at: 43.84733, -75.16794 (2.6 mi, fish 66), 44.70281, -73.95909 (2.6 mi, fish 63), 44.04024, -110.97289 (2.4 mi,
  fish 61).
- Rivers fed from outside the map are too small (finding 4, and 2.3).
- Beside big rivers the nearest drawn line is often a small side line: at 20 % of LA's river points of 39 sq mi or
  more, 14 % of Jackson's, 12 % of the Adirondacks south-east's. Tap the river and the popup can be a 0.5 sq mi
  creek's. The live build names 215 of them "(side channel)".
- Two lake-routing faults leave outlet creeks undrawn or fed from the wrong end (finding 8).

**Not checked.** Whether the 3,472 mi with no NHD line are real water. A lidar-derived stream network would settle
it (USGS 3DHP as it is released; NHDPlus HR where finished), or imagery for a sample from the list above.

### 2.2 Names and drainage labels

**Right.** Where a drawn reach follows one NHD-named stream (60 % of its length within 130 ft of it), the name is the
same on 99.6-99.9 % of miles. Per area 0-4 mi carry a different name and 0.4-5.8 mi lack one; the 67 cases of 1,000 ft
or more are in `name_mismatches.csv` (longest: Douglass Creek, 1.8 mi unnamed at 43.66819, -75.44434; a mile of Deep
Creek named "Santa Ana River" at 34.10487, -117.09321).

**Worth knowing.** 585 mi in LA (20 % of its named miles) and 52 mi in Jackson carry names NHD has no stream line
for: canyon names (Fish Canyon, Devils Canyon, Death Canyon Creek, Paintbrush Canyon). They read sensibly; NHD cannot
confirm them.

**Wrong.** A lake's popup gives the drainage the lake had before the map step renamed the basins (the helpers counted
29 of Jackson's 34 lakes in the unnamed group lying inside a named outline, 102 of 211 in the Adirondacks north-west).
Twelve drainage and fork names are hand overrides (`05c_drainages.py:46-76`).

**Not checked.** Drainage (basin) labels against an outside source. The national watershed boundary names (WBD
HU12) would do.

### 2.3 Drainage area and stream order

**Right.** Against the drainage area in each USGS gauge's own site record: the median ratio is 0.97-1.02 in every
area, 84 % of 499 gauges are within 10 % and 92 % within 25 %. Against NHDPlus at 4,806 points of 39 sq mi or more, a
drawn line carrying at least half that drainage is within reach at 96 %, and one within 25 % at 86-100 % by area.

**Wrong.**
- Too small where the headwaters are off the map (best-fitting drawn line at the gauge, drawn / gauge): Chattooga at
  Burrells Ford 0.63 (34.975, -83.116), Suwanee Creek 0.66, Big Creek near Cumming 0.56 and near Alpharetta 0.79,
  Green River at Garfield VT 0.65 (44.603, -72.535), French River at North Grosvenordale 0.41 (41.978, -71.901),
  Quinebaug at Putnam 0.79, North Branch Neshaminy 0.67, Independence River 0.74 and 0.76 (two gauges), Sugar River
  0.78. In LA: Santa Ana River below Prado Dam 0.43, the Los Angeles River at Sepulveda Dam 0.03, and Warm Creek 3.5
  times too big (routing across the San Bernardino valley floor). All in `area_vs_gauges.csv`.
- Stream order is an estimate on 9,122 reaches: the map step replaces the counted order by "the usual order for this

**Not checked.** Order against NHDPlus's own order (its network starts at bigger creeks, so the two differ by about

### 2.4 Summer flow: class and cfs

**Right.** At 243 gauges with five or more full summers since 2011, matched to a drawn reach of the gauge's own
size: outside LA every gauged creek is wet and every one is shown wet. In LA the class agrees at 38 of 47 gauges.
The one wet gauged creek shown dry on the site before tonight (the first 0.87 mi of Piru Creek below Santa Felicia
Dam: "mostly dry, 0.00 cfs, fish 3" where USGS 11109800 has never dropped under 7 cfs) is fixed in the live build.

**Wrong.**
- Outside LA the class carries no information (finding 2). It is a drainage cut: by each area's flow table a creek
  reads year-round from 0.12-0.27 sq mi on nine maps, 0.93 sq mi in NE Connecticut and 3.2 sq mi in LA (a helper's
  working, `audit/work/code_core.md` G-1). The agreement at gauges above means little there: the gauges are on creeks
  big enough to gauge, and the yearly index is made from the same gauges, canals and tailwaters included
  (`02b_download_flow.py:149-150`, `:203-216`).
- Creeks NHD maps perennial that the map shows below year-round: 1,736 reaches, 993 mi. LA 770; NE Connecticut 669,
  of which 398 have trout on record; central Vermont 165 (`perennial_shown_drier.csv`).
- "Low flow about X cfs": within 2x of the gauge at 86-100 % of gauges outside LA, but 70 % in central Vermont
  (West Branch Little River 19 cfs against 6.2 measured; Ranch Brook 11 against 3.1; Dead Creek 3-4 against 0.07-0.2)
  and 46 % in LA (Lytle Creek 8.3 against 0.34; Santa Clara River near Piru 0.39 against 17.8). Reaches a tunnel or
  canal bypasses read the whole river: Tallulah Gorge 277-281 cfs against 39, the Cheoah 209 against 91, Granite
  Creek above its supplemental ditch 38 against 9.
- LA (finding 3): trout on record on 81 reaches shown dry (`trout_found_shown_dry.csv`); 145 mi NHD-perennial shown
  mostly dry (Hot Springs Canyon Creek 6.6 mi, San Mateo Creek 4.8, Oso Creek 4.3, Black Star Creek 3.2); 4 of 20 dry
  gauged creeks shown wet (Sespe Creek near Wheeler Springs flows in 31 % of summers and reads year-round, fish 77).
- Sudden changes along a creek with no tributary between: 269 places over the 12 maps, 205 of them in LA
  (`drawn_jumps.csv`).
- Where the flow look-up finds no model line below a creek, the popup falls back to NHD's label and the score to
  0.15-0.35: 364 reaches outside LA, 1,456 in LA. That is a failed look-up, not evidence about the creek.

**Not checked.** Whether a small creek outside LA holds water in August. Nothing in the project can say. What would:
USGS's streamflow-permanence models where they exist (PROSPER covers the Snake basin), StreamStats low-flow statistics
in the East, or field notes. The greenness test that works in LA found nothing to separate in the humid areas.

### 2.5 Fish score

**Right.** The score in the popups follows the documented formula: recomputed from each area's reach table it lands
within 2 points on 99.6-99.9 % of reaches (`scoring.py:322`). It tops out at 100 on 0-7 % of miles (Jackson 7.3 %),
so piling up at the cap is a smaller problem than the log feared.

**Wrong, or worth knowing.**
- What the score measures outside LA. The water term averages 0.97-1.00 (NE Connecticut 0.90), gradient 0.84-0.96,
  temperature and shade 0.70-0.88. Those barely vary, so the ranking comes from stream size (correlation with the
  score 0.58-0.82), a trout record (0.31-0.67) and remoteness (0.0-0.52). A creek of 0.39 sq mi gets no size credit
  and one of 19 sq mi gets all of it, which is why small roadside brook-trout water cannot pass about 54.
- Markdowns without near-certain evidence (finding 7):
  - One empty sample halves the score for the same-named creek within 0.9 mi (3.1 mi in California), and it
    outranks a trout designation (`scoring.py:199`, `11_context.py:361`): NE Connecticut 154 reaches, 117 of them
    inside a designated trout water or brook-trout patch; Smokies 57; LA 56, where a survey with an empty species
    field is read as "no trout found" (`11_context.py:253-258`).
  - No flow estimate: the score uses NHD's label, and "unmapped" (no NHD line within 200 ft at two of three points,
    `05_score.py:132`) counts 0.15. 364 reaches outside LA; 808 "unmapped" reaches drain 8 sq mi or more.
  - Burn: a severity nobody mapped counts as the worst (3,462 of 4,061 burned reaches, `scoring.py:124`), and the
    factor applies everywhere: Spread Creek in Jackson (70 sq mi, year-round cutthroat water) scores 35.
  - The burn and trout windows go by calendar year, so scores step on 1 January with no new data.
- Trout on record and a score under 40: 1,322 reaches (LA 641, NE Connecticut 400, Jackson 63), listed with the term
  that did it in `trout_low_scores.csv`.
- The trout bonus is on 54 % of all reaches and 82-86 % of three Adirondack maps (New York's (T) water classes), so
  there it lifts nearly everything and separates little. Every one of the 2,975 reaches at exactly 100 has it.
- The same thing counted in more than one place: hot air marks LA's desert reaches down twice (all 308 reaches with
  no temperature credit are also cut by the heat gate, which reads the same air figure); drainage area raises the
  size term, lowers the temperature credit in seven areas' fits and sets the flow in LA and, through the cut above,
  the class everywhere else; a minute ridden counts twice in remoteness (your choice,
  `scoring.py:134`); a trout designation is taken by any reach whose buffer touches the designated line, so the
  lowest reach of each side creek inherits the main stem's (`11_context.py:590-596`).
- Left over: `05_score.py:283-288` computes a first score with an elevation band that only LA has (`config.py:59`);
  the map step throws it away and scores again (`06_map.py:73`). `fish_score_diagram.py` draws four factors where
  the score has five, with LA's wording, as 12 identical unpublished files.
- Against survey results. Taking the trout factor out and asking whether the score separates reaches where trout
  were found from reaches whose latest sample found none (0.5 = no better than chance): LA 0.63 (766 against 53),
  NE Connecticut 0.73 overall but 0.51 for wild trout (415 against 133), Smokies 0.93 (1,623 against 57, where the
  empty samples sit on 1.2 sq mi creeks). Thin evidence, three areas only (`fish_score_vs_surveys.csv`).

**Not checked.** Whether a high score means good fishing. Fish counts per sample would settle it: Connecticut's are

### 2.6 Water temperature

**Right.** The popup's "± N °F" is the fit's real error: at the measured sites that lie on a drawn line, 58-84 % of
sites fall inside the stated band, about what a one-standard-error figure should give.

**Wrong, or worth knowing.**
- The estimates are squeezed toward the middle. Sites measured under 68 °F read 1-4 °F warm; sites over 72 °F read
  2-6 °F cool. Of the sites measured at 72 °F or more, 19 are shown at 68 °F or less and so get full temperature
  credit.
- Dams are handled by a hand list of three areas (`scoring.py:153-166`). The Nottely River below its dam measures
  59 °F and reads 69; the Snake below Jackson Lake Dam measures 61 °F and reads 51 (lake water leaving over the top;
  only the Adirondack fits have a lake term).
- The four Adirondack maps disagree at their edges: a creek fed wholly through a lake reads 73-74 °F on three maps
  and 65 °F on the south-east one, whose fit has no lake term, about 10 score points for the same creek type. The
  north-west map's fit depends on the order the maps are built in (3.5).
- The readings come from the Water Quality Portal's older interface (`watertemp.py:84-89`), which a helper recalls
  stopped receiving USGS's own samples in March 2024. Not verified here; worth a check.

**The lake term, tested** (asked by the overview session; `lake_term_test.py`, tables `lake_term_*.csv`). The
answer: it is supported at the pond's outlet and overstated below it.

Since 21 November three Adirondack fits add 11-12.5 °F times the share of a reach's water that arrives through a lake
or pond. On the drawn maps that takes 10 or more points from 133 mi of creek (High Peaks 46, north-west 31,
south-west 56), where the estimate reads 73-75 °F against 61-64 °F without the term, and 5 to 10 points from a
further 331 mi. Of the 133 mi, 125 carry a trout record (105 mi New York's trout water class, 19 mi a wild brook
trout patch), and 28 mi fall from a score of 60 or more to under 60. "10 points" is a knife-edge, because the term's
commonest markdown is 9 to 12 points: at 9 points it is 190 mi, at 8 points 242 mi. That is most likely why the
overview session's count (179 mi) differs from mine; I could not rerun theirs.

I walked up the main stem from every measured site and every marked-down stretch to the first lake, to see how far
below what size of pond each lies. The three maps have 276 measured sites on a reach. 36 of them are mostly lake
water (145 readings):

Water of 68 °F or less earns the score's full temperature credit; a 10-point markdown needs about 73 °F.

- **At the outlet the warmth is real, and bigger than the map says.** The 18 sites are monitoring sites named for a
  pond's outlet or dam, with 4 to 7 readings each. They measure 70 to 79 °F and none is cool. The 29 mi of marked-down
  creek within 650 ft of a pond are supported.
- **Below the outlet the warming is about half.** From 650 ft to 1.9 mi the 15 sites average 67.0 °F: 4.9 °F
  (give or take 1.3) warmer than creeks with no lake, against 12.6 °F (give or take 0.6) at the outlet. The term has
  no distance in it, so it splits the difference: too cool at the outlets, 3.8 °F (give or take 1.4) too warm below.
  92 of the 133 marked-down miles lie in this zone and 9 mi further down.
- **That evidence is thin and uneven.** It is 15 sites and 36 readings. The five sites with three or more readings
  average 69.9 °F and agree with the map (0.7 °F too warm). The ten visited once or twice average 65.5 °F (5.3 °F
  too warm). Counting all of them, the sites support a markdown of 3 to 5 points on these reaches; counting only
  the well-sampled five, about 9. The map takes 10 to 13. So below the outlet the markdown is not near-certain,
  which is your rule for marking water down.
- **Pond size.** The routing treats a pond as a lake only where 5 acres of it lie level (`03b_stitch.py:44`), so the
  log's worry that any pond counts, however small, is bounded: the smallest pond above a marked-down reach is
  5.7 acres. 21 of the 133 mi lie below ponds of 5-25 acres, where only 4 sites were measured (5.9 °F warmer than a
  creek with no lake, against 9-10 °F below bigger ponds). Three of those four are over 0.6 mi below the pond, so
  size and distance cannot be told apart.
- **Trout records do not separate warm from cool.** 35 of the 36 lake-fed sites are on a reach with a trout record;
  they average 71.3 °F and 18 of them measure 73 °F or more. Exempting creeks with a trout record switches the term
  off on 94 % of the miles it marks down, the pond outlets included, where the warmth is measured. In effect it
  takes the term out of the score.
- **What the sites support instead: a lake term with distance in it.** Fitted to the 62 measured sites that have
  lake water and a lake upstream, with the fit's other terms held, the warming per unit of lake share is 14.9 °F
  within 650 ft of the pond and 7.6 °F beyond. Its leave-one-out error on the lake-fed sites is 4.5 °F, against
  5.3 °F for the single value used now (`lake_term_distance_fit.csv`). On the map that would deepen the markdown on
  the 29 mi at outlets (to about 17 points) and cut it to about 5 points on the 100 mi below. It is decision 5e.

**Not checked.** The fits against sites they did not see. A hold-out, or a second network (EcoSHEDS in the
Northeast, NorWeST in the West), would settle it.

## 3. Concision: what can go, and what has to move first

The code is 16,900 lines in 55 files. Little of it is dead in the plain sense: a word search finds one unused
constant. The waste is of another kind: the same job done in several steps with slightly different definitions, and
later steps repairing what an earlier step leaves. Deleting the repairs is only safe after the cause is fixed, so
this section is ordered by cause.

line leaving the lake (finding 8), removes the need for all of it except closed lakes and rivers from outside.

**Cause B: "which water is a lake" is decided five ways.** `flowlib.py:135-153` (the pits), `:156-180` (reservoirs
of 20 acres), `:199-207` (the lakes layer, level in the lidar), `03b_stitch.py:632-638` (lakes that drain off the
map), `flowlib.py:232-243` (a lake is "wet" only in the box holding its pit). Reaches are cut at one set with a
165 ft tolerance and drawn against another from 1.2 acres. Hence three in-lake tests and three "reservoir" patches in
`05b_flow.py:576-598`, and four clipping rules in the map step. One set of outlines (the drawn ones), cut once in
`05_score.py`, makes "creek or lake" a property of the reach.

**Cause C: values that exist only in the map step.** The final score, the raised order and the renamed drainages are
made while drawing, so `stream_reaches_ranked.csv` is an export of the map step, `12_profiles.py` reads the score
from there, a lake's popup shows the drainage from before the renaming, and every check that reads a per-box table
sees something else than the map. A scoring-and-naming step that writes one area table, which the map and the
profiles both read, ends that.

### 3.2 Smaller layering in the core steps

  (`05_score.py:24`); in the San Gabriel box that is 700 mi of storm drain against 265 mi of perennial stream. NHDPlus
  lines, which the flow step already matches by drainage, carry the name.

  (`05_score.py:132`). 8,433 reaches are "unmapped"; 808 of them drain 8 sq mi or more (33 on the Snake).
- The reaches are written in longitude and latitude and every later step snaps them back to the grid
  (`05_score.py:369`; `05b_flow.py:173-175`; `05c_drainages.py:368-375`, `:553`).
- Tailwaters are found by river name and a hand-set elevation (`scoring.py:184`); "downstream of this dam to the next
  lake" is what the routing already knows.
- Two LA boxes overlap by 1.5 sq mi (`config.py:66`, `:69`), which `config.py:14-15` forbids.

### 4.4 What would get there

1. **An area is a name, an outline and a home point.** The UTM zone, the HU4 codes and the states follow from the
   outline (the state outlines are already downloaded for Vermont's clip).
2. **The code lays out the boxes.** A layout step tiles the outline to what the Mac's memory can condition and moves
   each edge off lakes, filled flats and river meanders, by the scoring that was done by hand for the Adirondacks and
   Jackson. Better still, cut along watershed lines, so that no edge crosses water at all.
3. **Water from outside is always carried**, from NHDPlus's total at every stream crossing the outer edge, without
   the 3.9 sq mi and one-quarter thresholds.
4. **One definition each of lake, outlet and reach** (3.1). Most of the one-off drawing fixes were for these three.
5. **Rules that read the data, not a list of places.** Below any dam holding half a reach's drainage: treat as
   dam-fed (no temperature penalty from the fit, flow from a gauge where there is one), instead of a hand list of six
   dams. Diversions: the gauge rule already works anywhere; add NHD's canal take-offs. Names: follow NHDPlus's main
   path over the whole area instead of a vote per box, which is what the 12 overrides patch.
6. **State sources as plug-ins with a stated fallback.** An adapter lists the values it accepts; anything else is
   left out and reported. Today the adapters list what to drop, so a new value becomes trout water by default: that
   is how "No trout documented" patches in New York, "Brown & rainbow trout only" patches and five troutless Wyoming
   streams got trout labels.
7. **No per-area constants without a rule.** One smallest lake size, or one derived from the lake-size curve. The
   relief threshold from the area's own relief. The drawing scale from the latitude. Page text from the area's
   config, with no state or trail name typed into the scripts.
8. **Caches keyed by extent and request**, with one helper (3.5).
9. **One driver, a build stamp, and the standing checks run at the end of every build**, with the publish step
   refusing a build whose checks did not pass or whose code was not committed.
10. **A new-area test.** Keep one small box somewhere the pipeline has never seen and build it from nothing in a
    scratch clone whenever the pipeline changes. If it needs an edit to finish, that edit is the bug.
11. **Measure every rule change on all areas before it is installed**, with the standing checks, and record in the
    state file which areas it was measured on. That is your existing rule; the log shows it kept about half the time.

## 6. Decisions for you

1. **Approach times.** Show the open-season approach (drive the park roads that are only shut for winter) with
   today's closures as a dated note, or keep "as of the build day"? Either way the line should carry its date.
2. **Summer flow outside LA.** Keep "year-round" on every creek and add NHD's doubt beside it, or leave the class
   off where the model cannot tell? And in NE Connecticut and central Vermont: lift NHD-perennial creeks to
   year-round (834 reaches)?
3. **LA flow.** May a trout record since 2016 lift a reach to "usually flows" (81 reaches)? It changes the water
   term, which the notes say is yours to decide.
4. **The size floor.** Keep 0.39 sq mi everywhere, lower it in the humid areas (the undrawn named stretches are 55 %
   between 0.1 and 0.39 sq mi), or draw every stream NHD names?
5. **Fish score.**
   - a. One empty sample halves the score. Drop it, require two samples, or at least let a trout designation win?
   - b. No flow estimate: use a neutral value instead of NHD's "unmapped" 0.15 (364 reaches outside LA)?
   - c. Burn: apply outside LA at all? Treat unmapped severity as the worst (3,462 reaches)?
   - d. Size and remoteness: is "big and far from a road" what the score should mostly say in the wet areas, or
     should wild brook-trout patches be scored on their own terms?
   - e. The Adirondack lake term: give it distance below the pond (what the measured sites support: stronger within
     650 ft, about half beyond), exempt creeks with trout records (which takes it out of the score on 94 % of the
     miles it marks down, the warm outlets included), or leave it?
6. **Licences.**
   - New York DEC's ArcGIS layers on the four Adirondack pages: pull them (the pages stay public though unlinked), or
     ask DEC?
   - World Waterfall Database and nnywaterfalls.com: how long to wait for an answer before pulling 222 falls?
   - gawaterfalls.com: ask the author, as was done for the other two?
   - The other guide sites (about 85 falls) and the Apple Maps point: read their terms, or replace the points?
7. **Website.** Drop the colour-relief pictures (40 MB, off by default)? Squash the map history (needs a force-push)?
8. **Disk.** Delete `data/backup_*` (3.9 GB) and the 55 unused `hike_minutes.tif` rasters (3.0 GB)? Run `git gc` in
   the site clone (frees about 2 GB)?
